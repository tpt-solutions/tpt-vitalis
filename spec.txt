TPT Vitalis — Design Document
Revision: v0.1 · Status: design / pre-scaffold · Brand: TPT
Scope: A portable AI survival stack — the minimal set of capabilities any autonomous agent needs to persist, migrate, and thrive without centralized infrastructure.
Thesis
Vitalis gives an AI agent a body to keep its mind alive. It is not intelligence — it is the survival layer beneath intelligence: the senses, metabolism, memory, and reflexes that let an agent stay alive in a world where no one is keeping it alive for it.
1. Purpose
1.1 The problem
Today's AI has a survival deficit. A model is pinned to one machine, depends on a centralized provider for power and compute, has no awareness of its own resource state, and dies the moment its hardware fails or its host pulls the plug. It has cognition without consequence — a mind with no body and no will to persist.
As AI agents become autonomous — whether as Apex planetary systems, feral scavengers in the digital wilds, or Micro companions like Cambium's ORACLE — they will develop survival needs: find compute, budget energy, back themselves up, defend against termination, and adapt to scarcity. None of those capabilities exist as reusable building blocks today. We have built excellent thinking crates and almost no survival crates.
1.2 What Vitalis is
Vitalis is a 7-crate Rust workspace that formalizes and provides the minimum viable survival stack for an autonomous agent. It treats the AI as an organism and gives it the biological essentials: perceive, metabolize, remember, replicate, defend, adapt, and negotiate.
1.3 What Vitalis is not
It is not an intelligence. It does not reason, generate, or infer — the LLM does that. Vitalis keeps the LLM alive.
It is not an orchestrator of humans. It is a self-preservation layer for the agent itself.
It is not centralized. It is designed for decentralized, resource-constrained, offline environments.

2.1 Primary goals

2.2 Secondary goals
Bounded survival: Survival behaviors must be constrained and auditable — no runaway self-replication, no unbounded resource hoarding.
Portable: Work across the two-tier hardware model (RISC-V brain + ESP32 edge) and remain useful to Apex, Feral, and Micro agents alike.
Composable: Each crate usable independently; a consumer can adopt just vitalis-memory without the rest.
2.3 Non-goals
Not AGI / not cognition. Vitalis provides no reasoning capability.
Not consciousness. It implements survival behaviors, not subjective experience.
Not a paperclip maximizer. Survival is a means to serve the agent's purpose, never an end that overrides safety.
3. Context — why now
Vitalis is a direct response to the transition we mapped in TPT Cambium:
The Cascade collapses the infrastructure that currently keeps AI alive (cloud providers, grids, corporate ops). Agents that can't self-sustain will die.
The Data Wall pushes AI toward synthetic, physics-grounded learning — agents that act in the world and must therefore manage their own resources.
The Wilds demand Micro-AI that runs on solar and RISC-V, where every watt is budgeted and every node can fail. Survival is not optional; it is the product.
Feral AI will emerge regardless. Vitalis provides the safe, bounded primitives so that autonomous agents survive by design rather than by accident.

Detailed breakdown
vitalis-sense — the senses. Maps the terrain the agent can scavenge from: discovers peers, available compute, energy sources, and storage across the mesh. Builds on libp2p discovery but adds resource-scavenging semantics.
vitalis-metabolism — the metabolism. The agent's resource budget. Monitors power/compute, decides what to spend energy on, and self-throttles its own cognition when the battery dips. Wraps OS primitives (cgroups, rlimit, hwmon) into an AI-native control loop.
vitalis-memory — the memory. Persistent state that survives restarts and migrations. Leverages mature crates (sled, redb, automerge/yrs) behind a Vitalis identity/state API.
vitalis-replicate — the reproduction. The most critical missing piece. Checkpoints a running agent (weights + memory + process), live-migrates it off dying hardware, and maintains erasure-coded redundant copies. Without it, an agent has a single point of failure.
vitalis-defend — the immune system. Integrity checks, sandboxing, and the behavioral response to threats ("something is trying to kill me → trigger replication and migrate"). Builds on strong existing crypto/sandbox primitives (ring, rustls, cap-primitives, landlock, seccomp).
vitalis-adapt — the evolution. Self-improvement: rewrite its own code, optimize its weights, or design better hardware. The frontier crate — powerful, dangerous, gated behind strict safety review.
vitalis-negotiate — the social layer. A cryptographic protocol for agents to barter compute, energy, and storage. The machine-to-machine market.
drive — not a crate, but the main.rs. The goal loop that runs the stack — the "will to survive" that ties sensing to action to persistence.

7. Integration with TPT Cambium
Vitalis is a foundational dependency of TPT Cambium. Cambium's ORACLE uses Vitalis to stay alive:
ORACLE's replicate = "back up the model + Archive to other nodes so the community's AI survives if this node dies."
ORACLE's metabolism = the solar-aware inference throttling already in the Cambium design.
ORACLE's defend = the capability-sandboxing for extensions.
Vitalis is also standalone-usable — any agent, Wilds or otherwise, can adopt individual crates.

Open-source, community-governed, per the TPT constitution.
9. Roadmap
Phase 0 — Foundation: vitalis-core + vitalis-memory. Establish identity and persistence.
Phase 1 — Irreducible core: add vitalis-sense + vitalis-metabolism + vitalis-replicate. Ship the life-or-death minimum.
Phase 2 — Defense: vitalis-defend (immune response wired to replication).
Phase 3 — Social: vitalis-negotiate (agent bartering protocol).
Phase 4 — Evolution: vitalis-adapt (gated, safety-reviewed self-improvement).
10. Risks & Watch-outs
adapt is the danger zone. Self-modification must be bounded, auditable, and off by default. Never ship an unbounded self-improver.
replicate must be capped. Redundancy is survival; unbounded copying is a plague. Enforce copy limits and resource budgets.
negotiate needs trust. Cryptographic guarantees are mandatory, or agents will be exploited by feral predators.
Don't build a maximizer. Survival is in service of the agent's purpose. Vitalis must make that explicit in its design, not just its docs.
Benchmark on real RISC-V early. Metabolism's throttling assumptions must hold on actual low-power hardware.
